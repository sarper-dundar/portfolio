# The Marnok — Fungal Sentience

The Marnok were never meant to be people. They were equipment.

In a world that no longer exists, they were combat enhancers — spores that spread across a warrior's body and amplified strength, speed, and whatever else that particular strain was good at. Then the war came, the world was destroyed, and the spores found themselves inside hosts who were nearly dead. So they adapted. They learned to work a dying body, then a dead one, then how to move to another. Over generations they built societies in the Forgotten Arteries beneath Barakorn out of the bodies and tools of whatever came through the tunnels.

Everything interesting about them comes from that origin. They aren't a species that evolved into intelligence. They're a product that outlived its manufacturer and kept running.

The caste structure is the clearest example. Marnok society divides into warrior, worker, and scholar castes, and it isn't a political arrangement — it's residue. Different spore strains enhanced warriors differently in the old world, and those tendencies never went away. A spore is drawn to a role because of what it was designed to do for someone else thousands of years ago. Their entire social order is a specification sheet nobody can read anymore.

Their leaders are the **Elders**: spores that took hosts with very long lifespans and accumulated knowledge from the old world across centuries. They're revered, consulted before every major decision, and function as the race's memory. When an Elder dies its accumulated knowledge passes into the spores of younger Marnok, so nothing is lost. This is also why they have no writing system — they never needed one, because the storage medium is alive and passes itself on.

How a Marnok "person" works is the question I got asked most, and the answer is that it varies by kingdom, deliberately. Some kingdoms pursue genuine symbiosis and leave the host conscious with shared control. Some take partial control and leave the host diminished. Some take everything. It's cultural rather than biological, which means the Marnok have an ongoing ethical argument with themselves about what they're allowed to do to the people they wear, and different kingdoms have landed in different places.

The variant that unsettles me most is the willing hosts. It's rare, but a sufficiently skilled spore can project sweet dreams or pleasant illusions to keep a valuable host content inside the hive. Not every spore can manage it, and sometimes it takes several working together. A host kept that way isn't suffering. That's the problem.

Communication runs on spore strings — nearly invisible threads released into the air that carry thought between Marnok in proximity. It isn't telepathy and it has range limits, but it produces something close to a hive mind: individuals with free will who become collectively sharper whenever they're near each other. A lone Marnok is a competent schemer. Six of them in a room are a strategist. They speak to outsiders in the languages of the hosts they've taken, which is its own quiet horror — you are being addressed in a dead person's voice, fluently.

Capability transfers with the host. Take someone alive and you keep almost everything they could do. Take them dead and what you inherit is degraded. That single rule drives their entire hunting doctrine: they don't want food, they want _skills_. They feed adequately off surface plant roots and hunt for knowledge and tools instead, and they'd much rather capture a competent stranger than kill one. It also means a small number of people with natural resistance or outright immunity to the spores are enormously valuable in the Arteries, and make their living as traders and guides on that basis.

Their weakness is heat and sunlight. They adapted to damp darkness and even moderate light disrupts them, which is why they hold the tunnels and avoid the surface, and why they're most vulnerable during the day. They have no light-shielding technology in this age. They are, in my notes, actively working on it.

What I like about running them is that they're aggressive and patient at the same time. They want to expand quickly and their shrewdness compels them to do it efficiently, so every move gets calculated first — and they're usually right. They avoid tangling with the world's larger factions and pick off lost traders instead, which makes them a persistent background threat rather than a war. The Arteries are dangerous because something down there is thinking carefully about you and is in no hurry.
