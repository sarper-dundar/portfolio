# Dream Magic and Ertene's Dream

Ertene breathed life into the cosmos, set it moving, and then went to sleep. He is still asleep. The dimension his sleep produced is called Ertene's Dream, and it is the only magic system in Kazankor where the practitioner has to travel to the place where the magic is.

That's the design decision the whole system hangs on. The other four systems bring power to the practitioner — inscribed, sung, brewed, or borrowed. Dream Magic doesn't. You go there. Which means every Dream Magic session is a location as much as a resource, and it can't be used to solve a problem in the room you're standing in without a plan for the fact that you'll be lying down.

The resource is belief. **Dream Points** measure the strength of a dreamer's will and the clarity of their mind, and the starting pool is just Wisdom plus Charisma modifier — deliberately small, because I didn't want a level-one dreamer capable of much. You regain them inside the Dream rather than outside it: a couple of points for meditating there, three for confronting and overcoming a fear, and half your maximum for resting within the Dream itself. That last one is the quiet lever. A dreamer who wants to operate at capacity has to be willing to sleep inside the thing.

Spending is straightforward — one or two points for a minor effect, three to five for a moderate one, six and up for something large. What makes it interesting is that spending isn't sufficient. The Dream resists. Every effect also needs a **Mental Strength Check** against a Belief DC, using your best mental stat: 10 to change your own appearance or move an object, 15 for a minor illusion or an environmental shift, 20 to reshape terrain, 25 for a genuine reality alteration. Miss by more than five and the Dream pushes back with a **Backlash** that reflects the dreamer's own fears rather than anything external. The terrain is made of minds, so when it retaliates it retaliates with yours.

Mastery is tracked by successful use rather than by level, which I did to keep Dream Magic from advancing on the same clock as everything else. Ten successes make you an Apprentice, fifty a Journeyman, a hundred an Adept, two hundred a Master. The Master tier is where it stops being a utility system: a Master can anchor themselves to specific Dream locations, and then physically teleport to them in the waking world. Anchoring takes five visits to the same place or one complex effect landed there. The teleport itself costs ten Dream Points and a DC 25 check, once a day, and failing shrinks your maximum pool until you fully rest. A Dreamwalker who has spent a campaign building anchors has quietly constructed a private transit network.

Because the Dream is made of everyone's minds, it isn't empty. Nightmares form out of mortal fear and hunt dreamers who wander. Guardians of Sleep, born from Ertene's own slumbering mind, look for imbalances and for individuals who are becoming dangerous — which is worth remembering, because a player who gets good at this eventually qualifies. Every living thing touches the Dream whether or not it knows, and some people cross over by accident and don't realise where they are.

The rule governing what carries back is the one I spent longest on, and it ended up simple: **the Dream reaches souls, not matter.** Injuries taken there are real injuries. Kill a person in the Dream and they are dead in the waking world. But a thing without a living soul is untouchable — you can level a building in the Dream and the building in the world stands exactly as it did, undamaged and unmoved. There is no dream route to demolishing a wall, forging a key, or stealing an object.

That line does two jobs at once. It makes the Dream lethal enough that nobody treats it as a consequence-free sandbox, and it stops it becoming a universal solvent for physical problems. A Dreamwalker cannot open a locked vault from bed. They can only reach the person holding the key. On top of the hard rule, ordinary failure still costs something smaller — a bad Backlash leaves Dream Wounds that carry as psychological effects, disadvantage on social and insight checks for a stretch, while a landed complex effect leaves the dreamer sharpened for a day.

The broader effect of the system is on information rather than combat. Dreamers can spy, project across distance, and influence other people's dreams to move them in the waking world. A campaign with a Dreamwalker in it has a very different intelligence economy than one without, and that has been more disruptive to my planning than any amount of damage output.
