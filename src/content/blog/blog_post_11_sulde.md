# Sülde — Elemental Bending Through Pacts

Sülde is not magic, and the reason is that the power isn't in the bender.

Every element in Kazankor is made of **Iye** — small living spirit-beings. Every flame is a gathering of fire-Iye. A mountain is a slow conversation of earth-Iye that has been going on since the world was shaped. A bender doesn't channel a field or impose a pattern. They ask, and something alive answers.

That premise settled the mechanics almost by itself, because once the elements are people, the price of an action should depend on what you're asking them to do. **Yarat** (Create) summons local Iye to gather where you point, at two Sülde per cube. **Değiştir** (Change) asks Iye already present to take a new form — the smallest imposition, one per cube. **Bilgi Topla** (Gather Knowledge) is just listening, also one, and it never deals damage; it gives you history, identity, secrets. And **Yok Et** (Destroy), which evicts Iye from where they are, costs three — the most expensive category in the system, for a reason written directly into the rules: the Iye resist. The pricing isn't balance-first. The cheapest thing to ask is the smallest favour, and the dearest is the one they don't want to do.

Every category of every element levels independently, which makes the character sheet a skills matrix rather than a spell list. Fire Yarat at level 5 beside Fire Yok Et at level 2 describes a bender who creates beautifully and can't unmake anything. Nothing transfers between elements, so a specialist always beats a generalist.

The choice at creation I like most is cosmetically small and thematically total. Wisdom or Charisma, mechanically identical. Wisdom benders **listen** to Iye and persuade through attunement. Charisma benders **compel** them through force of personality. Same numbers, opposite relationship.

Environment matters because Iye density is real. A forge swarms with fire-Iye and bending there is cheap. A frozen tundra costs more. Some places will shut a low-level bender down entirely.

At the top, the system stops being a resource and becomes a relationship. Higher-order Iye accumulate in rich places over enormous spans and become **named** conscious beings larger than any human mind — Kızılbaş beneath Mount Tostur, Denizana in the eastern gulf, Taşyürek in the Spine. None are combat encounters. They're negotiation partners, and a pact is permanent. Getting one isn't a check, either. To pact with Kızılbaş you climb Tostur on the night after a new moon and keep a fire alive until dawn. Around the third hour he asks what you want the fire for, and he knows if you lie.

Underneath all of it is something I've kept out of the players' hands. Higher-order Iye know about the harvest. Every reset scatters their gatherings and forces them to re-accumulate over millennia, and they remember — Taşyürek has lived through at least two. That makes them the only faction with both a grievance against the Luminous Ones and a form the gods can't easily suppress, and a bender holding a major pact is a possible route for them to act through. It's a long game and I haven't tipped it.

The cost of building Sülde this way is that it doesn't run itself. Five categories across several elements is a wide tracking surface, and the system is only as good as a DM willing to play the Iye as characters with memory and opinions. Run them as a resource and it's a fiddly damage system. Run them as people and it's the most sociable magic in the setting.
