# Kharos-Vaul: A City Built for Overpowered Parties

At some point every long campaign hits the problem where the party can beat the setting. They have teleportation, scrying, resurrection, and enough damage to remove any individual obstacle. Cities stop being places and become a list of guards with the wrong numbers on them.

Kharos-Vaul is my answer. I have two groups running in this world — one around level 2 and one around level 9 that is frankly overpowered for its level — and the brief I wrote for the second group was narrow: a city they can enter, work in, and eventually break, but cannot simply _walk through_.

It's a city-sized war engine carved into a volcanic caldera — the empire's greatest factory, its arcane brain-trust, and the anchor of everything it plans to conquer next. That framing did most of the work, because a place built to project force is inherently built to resist it, and every defensive feature has an in-world reason to exist that has nothing to do with the players.

The first structural decision was the objective. Razing buildings isn't a win condition here. To actually end Kharos-Vaul, four things have to go: the **Command Triad** — High Marshal, Spymaster, Arch-Artificer — with no survivors capable of reorganising; the **arcane genome**, meaning the libraries, the academy staff, the unique golem designs and war-spells that give the empire its edge; the **weird arsenal** of secret caches; and the **geopolitical position** itself, which means rendering the caldera unusable for a generation. Four objectives, four different skill sets, none of them solvable by damage alone. A party that can kill anything still has to work out what killing accomplishes.

The second decision was that its destruction has to be surgical. Brute force triggers a cascade that burns the continent and directly threatens everything the party has built elsewhere. That's the single most useful line in the document, because it converts overwhelming power from an advantage into a hazard. The strongest option available to them is also the one that costs them the most.

The third — and this is the one I'd carry into any other city — is that every district has a **public face and a hidden spine**. Each one lists what any competent spy can observe, and then separately what only the Triad and their inner circles know. So reconnaissance genuinely works: a party that scouts well learns the visible layer accurately and completely. It just isn't the whole thing.

My favourite example is small. The perimeter's anti-teleportation keystones are publicly known; everyone in the empire knows teleporting in requires a Rune Key. What isn't known is that three of the visible menhirs are decoys. Destroying them accomplishes nothing except alerting the Spymaster, while the real keystones sit thirty feet underground behind traps that drop saboteurs into magma pockets. A party that did its homework, executed cleanly, and hit the wrong stones has failed without a single roll going against them.

The districts each carry their own character. The **Caldera Rim** handles anything airborne. The **Bitter Anchorage** is the harbour and the fleet's staging ground. The **Foundry Ring** is military industry, powered by something that should not be powering anything. The **Aegis Citadel** is command, the teleport nexus for the whole frontier, and home to the Imperial War Banner. The **Shrouded Spire** is the mage academy and black operations, and it's the most secure place in the city by a wide margin. Around and beneath those sit the lived-in parts — the Crown District, the Artisan Ladder, the Ashfall Slums, and the Warrens underneath everything.

The slums are a deliberate honey trap, which is the sort of detail that tells you what kind of city this is. The academy is the most secure district and the poorest one is the least, and that asymmetry is intentional on the empire's part, not an oversight on mine. It's easier to watch people who think they're unwatched.

There's one revision I'd flag as a genuine design lesson. My first version topped out at level 30 — the Triad and the harbour and the foundry's bound elemental were all at the absolute ceiling of what a mortal can be. I scaled the whole city down to a cap of 27. Not because it was too hard, but because a city where the local officials are the most powerful beings in existence leaves no room for anything worse elsewhere in the world. Kharos-Vaul should be the deadliest place the party has been. It should not be the deadliest place there is.
