# Tözyar — The Plane of Fundamental Forces

Most settings split time magic, teleportation and gravity into separate schools. Tözyar folds them into one plane on purpose, and the reason is that in Kazankor they are one job: holding the structure of reality together so it keeps working.

Tözyar isn't a place in any way a mortal would recognize. It's the framework the other planes are built on — timeless, dimensionless, where beginning and end and large and small stop meaning anything. Its inhabitants match. The **Essentii** are constructs of raw cosmic force, each bound to one force and working in concert with the rest. The **Guardians of Flow** protect the integrity of time and space specifically. Neither group cares about anything happening in the world. They exist in a state of detachment and intervene only when the forces themselves go wrong, at which point they correct the disturbance with no interest in who caused it or why.

The plane's owner is **Kazagol, the Binder of Realities** — and Plane Owners in Kazankor are not gods who govern a territory. Each one _is_ their plane expressed as a being, which is why a plane's condition tracks its owner's state rather than following its owner's orders. Kazagol is the least dramatic god I've written, deliberately. He appears as a shifting mass of stars and dust, sometimes humanoid, usually not. He has no ambition. He does not want anything. He does not care about the affairs of gods or mortals and he is not being coy about it — the stability of time, space and gravity is his entire interest, and if you aren't threatening that, you don't exist to him. That's what makes him useful in play. A party can't bargain with Kazagol because there is nothing he wants, and they can't provoke him unless they break something structural, at which point he arrives without warning or negotiation.

What I like most about Tözyar is the contact rituals, because they don't involve asking. They involve demonstrating that you understand what the plane governs by submitting to it.

The Ritual of the Moment of Stillness is an hour of absolute physical stillness — no moving, no speaking, not even blinking on purpose, mind clear of everything except the passage of time. During it the caster feels seconds stretch into minutes and the world go distant. That's the plane testing whether they can take its influence. The Ritual of Gravity's Offering has the caster lie flat and pile twenty or thirty pounds of stone and metal on their chest, arms and legs, then meditate on the weight until they accept it. The Ritual of Dimensional Focus surrounds them with objects at fixed distances for two hours until they can feel the space between things.

None of those are checks against a monster. They're a person making themselves briefly subject to time, weight and distance in order to be allowed to touch them. It's the tone I want the whole Outsource system to have.

The mechanics carry the same idea. A practitioner can build a **Reality Tether** — a one-minute ritual anchoring themselves to a real, stable object — which cuts backlash by a quarter. The price is that you give up a sense, or a point of Strength or Dexterity, or your short-term memory retention, for the duration. And you cannot move more than ten feet from the anchor. Safety in this plane means being pinned in place, which is exactly the trade Tözyar should offer.

Push harder and you get **Gravity Flux**. Every high-cost spell adds to a running chance that gravity in a thirty-foot radius simply stops behaving: everything twice as heavy, or weightless and drifting upward, or pulling sideways, or pulsing in and out of normal. It doesn't discriminate. The caster's allies are standing in that radius too, which makes Tözyar the plane where the party's own support is a hazard.

The prices Tözyar charges are the ones that fit it: temporal perception, spatial awareness, balance, the memory of a place. Practitioners lose their sense of _where_ and _when_ before they lose anything else, and I've found that unsettles players more than losing hit points ever does. A character who can bend time and no longer reliably knows what day it is has paid something they can feel.

One connection worth noting for later: Tözyar keeps watch over Ulgarden. Knowledge is bound to chronology, so uncontrolled time manipulation would corrupt the archive. The two planes are permanently linked by that dependency, which is the closest thing my cosmology has to bureaucracy.
