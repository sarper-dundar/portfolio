# The Abyssal Vault

The Abyssal Vault is a permanent prison in the void between dimensions, anchored to nothing — not Kazankor, not any of the nine planes, not any region of space a normal being can reach. It's older than the current cycle, older than the planar order, and by some accounts older than the documented existence of the Atalar.

It holds prisoners of every category. Mortals who broke laws of cosmic significance. Tözen who performed Forbidden workings. Gods who fell out of favour with their pantheons or with the primordials. Beings from cosmologies that border this one, caught doing things any cosmic order would punish. And things that fit no category at all, which some primordial decided needed removing from the cosmos without being destroyed.

Souls bound here are bound permanently. They don't die in the ordinary sense, don't pass through Tünmorin's cycle, and don't reincarnate. They persist in their cells for as long as the Vault stands, which so far is indefinitely.

The origin is the part that makes it work as a piece of setting rather than a location. In deep cosmic time — before the planar order was established — there was a war among the primordials, multiple Atalar pantheons fighting across what would become this region. Nobody won. It ended in a settlement, and one of that settlement's provisions was that there had to be a _neutral facility_ for beings dangerous enough to require permanent isolation. It couldn't be owned by any pantheon, couldn't be controllable, couldn't be removable. It had to exist forever, independent of any primordial's continued goodwill.

To make that possible, one of the warring primordials volunteered to **become the facility**. One of the oldest, present at the war's beginning and worn down by its duration. They offered themselves as its sustaining attention and structural anchor. The others accepted on the condition that the bound primordial could never be removed, released, or permitted to refuse the role for as long as the Vault stood. The primordial accepted that too.

That's **the Keeper** — Düğüm Atası, the Knot-Father — and their continuous attention is what makes the Vault exist. Without it the place dissolves. They can't leave, and they can't use their power for anything outside it. A being peer to the Atalar, powerful in the way primordials are powerful, with all of that power bound to one job they agreed to do forever because the alternative was the war continuing.

The Vault's second function is the one that gets players inside it. Gods send their most trusted followers in as training. Confronting a prisoner in their pocket dimension, defeating them, surviving it, and learning from what was witnessed strengthens a mortal in ways ordinary combat can't. It's a harsh teacher and many who enter don't come back.

What I like is that the two functions aren't in tension. The same act that punishes the prisoner — being confronted and beaten in the privacy of their personal hell — is what trains the attacker. It was designed that way. A prison that doubles as a trial ground isn't a compromise between two ideas; it's one idea seen from two sides.

Getting in is genuinely hard, and the five routes each carry their own story. **Divine sponsorship** is the intended path — a god grants permission to a follower they trust, as a token or a coded passage or a direct introduction to a portal the mortal couldn't otherwise approach. **Information brokers** sell portal locations for sums that would bankrupt a small kingdom, or for a service they consider equivalent. **Inheritance** covers the rare lineages that kept the knowledge from a sponsored ancestor and guard it above everything else. **Theft** draws attention from the Vault's operators before the thief even arrives. And **accident**, which is documented and vanishingly rare: people who stumble through a portal whose concealment has degraded, arrive unprepared, die quickly, and become part of the floor they landed on.

Then there's the toll, and it's the detail I'd point at if someone asked what makes the Vault feel like a real institution. Entry costs **Düğüm Taşı** — knot-stones, the Vault's own currency — paid to a Counter at the threshold, scaling with depth. Five stones for the upper floors. Fifteen for floor 100. A hundred and thirty-five for floor 250. Over a thousand for the deep floors, where the price is set personally and access may be refused regardless of payment.

And crucially: someone starting their delve at a deeper floor pays the cumulative cost of every floor above it. That single rule is why almost everyone begins at floor one and works down. A divine sponsor typically buys their follower entry to one specific floor and expects them to fight their way deeper on their own — which is a very clear statement about what the sponsorship is actually for.

The design lesson I took from building it is that an eternal prison is a much better location when you know who's paying for it. The Vault isn't a dungeon that exists because dungeons exist. It's the physical form of a peace treaty, staffed by the one participant who agreed to stop being a person so the others could stop fighting.
