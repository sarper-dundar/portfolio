# The Forgotten Arteries — Layered Cities Below

Beneath Barakorn and other lands run the Forgotten Arteries: ancient winding tunnels through the remnants of worlds that are gone. Descending means finding older roads and crumbling cities built layer on layer by civilizations nobody remembers, along with the Tamutash and Agyntash that make the whole place worth the risk of never coming back up.

The structural decision that made the Arteries work was making each floor a different _age_ rather than a different difficulty. Depth isn't a number here. It's a date.

**Floor 1 is the recent dead**, and it isn't a civilizational collapse at all — it's pieces of the current age that fell in. Individual buildings, districts, sections of cities that still exist above. Often the surface city corresponding to a Floor 1 node is standing right now, because the collapse happened underground without touching what was on top. Which means many Floor 1 nodes have working connections to the surface that the people up there don't know about.

That makes the first floor a genuinely different kind of danger. Its hazards are often active city infrastructure — water pipes, heating vents, structural supports — so damaging something down here can have consequences above, and a defense that collapses a section or floods a chamber is indistinguishable from an environmental accident until you understand the connection. The real threats aren't built defenses either; they're the people who moved in. Experienced scavengers with years in specific nodes. Criminal organisations using collapsed districts for exactly what collapsed districts are useful for. Marnok scouts whose territory includes coordination between the surface and the tunnels.

My favourite thing on Floor 1 is the Enforcement Pit: a municipal prison that became permanent when its shaft collapsed, where three generations of prisoners have built a functioning community of about two hundred with their own economy and justice system. They've also been exploring downward, which makes them the most experienced Floor 1 navigators alive, holding a three-generation oral history of everything that has ever come up from below and what it did.

**Floor 2 is the Machine Age** — a global civilization at its peak, twenty thousand years dead, everything mechanical and everything inscribed and everything still moving. Its residents are the Konstrukts, ancient machines working to restore a Central AI that is distributed rather than singular: its core sits in one node, but fragments live in terminal stations across every Floor 2 site in the world, isolated for twenty millennia and developed independently. No two fragments answer the same question the same way.

Floor 2's defenses are the best argument I have for building ruins as systems rather than encounters. The Patrol Units remember: one encountered today recalls every creature it has ever processed as authorized, unauthorized, or unknown, and it shares that with every other unit it meets. Handle one badly and you arrive at a node on the other side of the world already flagged.

Better still are the inscription arrays. The industrial Tamga covering Floor 2 includes defense sections built to respond to specific threat profiles — and after twenty thousand years without recalibration, those profiles have drifted. An array built to detect one enemy faction's True Source signature may now trigger on any signature above a threshold. An array meant to stop unauthorized access has expanded its definition of unauthorized to include anyone not cleared by a system that no longer runs. Parts of Floor 2 have been hostile to absolutely everyone for the entire time since the civilization ended, and nothing down there knows it's malfunctioning.

**Floor 3 is the Bright Age**, forty thousand years down, and it's where the Sönmezler come from. It has objects with minds in them — not haunted, deliberately built that way. A weapon designed to accept one specific wielder and refuse everyone else has been waiting for that person for forty thousand years and has had all that time to develop opinions about the wait. A vault built to open for particular biological credentials has been logging every unauthorized attempt since, and the log is now forty millennia long. The vault finds the record interesting and will discuss it.

Its living population is the Radiant Remnants, held in stasis by the Source Springs, and they come with the cleanest tragic constraint I've written: move one more than forty-eight hours from a Spring and the stasis breaks, and they age forty thousand years in about seventy-two hours. They know. It's the first thing they say if you wake them — _you cannot take me with you._ Any real relationship with them means staying on Floor 3 or solving that problem.

Below that the floors keep going, into the Age of Wells and deeper, where the Marnok kingdoms sit.

Two things tie it together. Scale is deliberately incomprehensible: exploring a Floor 3 node means exploring _one_ city or installation or laboratory from that age, not the floor, which would take decades. And the floors talk to each other. Konstrukt materials show up in Floor 1 infrastructure, scavenged from ruins above — the Konstrukts consider this theft and they keep a list. The Machine Age was building an unfinished project that needed a mineral from Floor 4, a technique from Floor 3, and Marnok design documents from Floor 5, collecting downward from the floors beneath them when the reset arrived. The Central AI has been waiting twenty thousand years for someone to finish it.

That last detail is the one that opens onto the next post. Every civilization down there was reaching toward the ones below it, and every one of them ran out of time on roughly the same schedule.
