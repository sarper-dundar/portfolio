# The Rüzgar Prestige — Wind Sülde

The line I wrote at the top of the Rüzgar document is the whole design: _the Rüzgar does not command the air; the air has simply decided to walk beside them._

Everything in the class had to follow from that. A bender who commands wind gets more damage. A bender the wind has chosen to accompany gets something else, and working out what that something else was turned into the most useful exercise I've done on prestige design.

The prerequisites set the terms. You need an active Wind Iye pact, meaning you're already a Sülde bender with wind as a practiced element. You need real depth — category level 4 or better in at least two of the five wind categories, so this isn't available to a dabbler. And you need a defining moment of trust, agreed with the DM: a scene where the bender chose the wind over some safer or stronger option, and the Iye chose to stay.

That third one is the actual gate. The numbers just prove you've been doing the work. The scene proves the relationship exists, and no amount of grinding produces it.

Then the levels, and here's where it gets unusual. **Yoldaş Rüzgar** at first level is a companion wind that supports your movement — faster walking, no difficulty from natural ground, no losing your footing to wind or current or an unstable surface. Useful. Modest. And explicitly still a request: the note in my document says the wind is willing, but it is still being asked, because it hasn't yet decided to stay on its own.

**Rüzgar Topları** at second level summons small spheres of living air, each carrying a sliver of the Iye's attention. They have one hit point, cannot attack, and dissolve on any solid hit. What they do is carry light objects, fetch and deliver, open unbarred doors, hold tools, turn pages, sweep, and run errands within sight.

I gave a prestige class a second-level ability that does housework, and I'd do it again. It's the clearest statement in the whole document about what this class is. The wind is _with_ you now, and what a companion actually does most of the time is help with ordinary things.

**Rüzgarın Eşliği** at third level is advantage on Performance checks whenever there's open air, because when the Rüzgar sings or plays or recites, the wind joins in — carrying the voice further, turning the pages, lifting a cloak at the right moment. Third-level ability. Not a combat feature. A relationship feature.

Only at fourth level does it start to bite, and even then it does so quietly: **Rüzgarla Hareket** adds the character's proficiency bonus to the highest Sülde value used in any movement-based wind working. The framing matters — wind-work is no longer something they do, it's how they move.

**Kırılan Hava** at fifth is the payoff. Every living thing in motion breaks the air around it, and the Rüzgar has learned to feel those broken currents — the eddies trailing a moving body, the still pocket where somebody is holding their breath. They cannot be surprised by anything that moves through or breathes the air near them, and the DM can extend that to locating a hidden creature through the disturbance it makes, without line of sight.

That's a genuinely strong defensive ability, and it arrives at the end, and it's still not a damage increase. It's perception. The wind tells them what it touched.

What makes a prestige feel earned, on the evidence of this one, is three things.

It has to be **specific enough to exclude**. This class is useless to anyone who isn't a wind bender with a pact, and I didn't try to broaden it.

It has to **cost something to qualify for**, and the cost has to be a decision rather than an accumulation. The moment where you chose the wind over the stronger option is the price, and it was paid in play, probably at a cost you felt at the time.

And it has to **change what the character is** rather than what their numbers are. A Rüzgar can be identified across a room by someone who knows what to look for, because the air behaves differently around them. Their laundry is done by wind. They perform better in the open than indoors. Half the class is texture and the texture is the point.

The honest tradeoff is that a player who wanted a wind-themed damage boost will find the Rüzgar disappointing through its first three levels, and they'd be right to. This is a class for someone who has already decided the relationship with the Iye is the interesting part of their character. For anyone else it's a slow start and a good ending, and I'd rather build one that filters that way than one that appeals to everyone.
