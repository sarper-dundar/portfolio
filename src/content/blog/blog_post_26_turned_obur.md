# The Turned Obur

My first version of the Turned was lazy. Mortals transfused with Obur blood, weaker than the real thing, essentially diluted vampires. It worked and it was boring, and once I understood what the Obur actually were it stopped being defensible.

Here's the revised answer, and it's stranger. A Turned Obur is a mortal who has been forcibly awakened to True Source by an Obur's channeling, who lacks the Wilder talent to sustain that channeling on their own, and who therefore channels **through their master** as a mediated conduit. They aren't diluted. They're plugged in.

That single change fixed everything I disliked about the original. The master-servant relationship stopped being a social convention and became a physical fact. The Turned's dependence isn't loyalty or addiction — it's plumbing.

The making of one is a ritual across several nights, not a transfusion. First the **Drawing**: the senior Obur presents their own blood and the mortal must drink it willingly, in full knowledge of what's happening. Forced turnings exist, are forbidden in most clans, and almost always fail — which I like as a rule, because it means the horror here requires consent and can't be inflicted casually. Then the **Vein-Linking** across several nights, where Sanguine Power passes into the mortal in measured amounts, restructuring their body to accept what's coming. Then the **Sealing** on the final night, which completes the binding permanently.

Each stage can fail, and the failures get worse as you go. Fail the Vein-Linking and you die slowly over weeks. Fail the Sealing and you become something worse than dead — a body already changing with the binding left incomplete. Those cases are rare and uniformly tragic, and most clans destroy them out of mercy. There's no reversal written anywhere in the process. The only outcomes are Turned, dead, or that.

Who gets turned varies by clan, and the categories say a lot about which clan you're dealing with. Volunteers who've served for years and choose it knowingly. Selected loyalists offered it as both reward and leash. Survivors of heavy feeding whose bodies are already partly primed — more common in the eastern clans, which my notes describe as less precious about consent. And a few clans that turn specific bloodlines generation after generation to keep one family in service, where each generation's Turned dies before a replacement is needed and the family knows what's coming and treats it as an inheritance.

Physically they sit between. The heart slows, though not to Obur rhythm, so they read as calmer than their temperament would otherwise allow — their pulse simply won't race. Their eyes take on a reddish or amber tinge that deepens when their master is nearby or when they're using what they've been given. Their metabolism runs hotter than mortal biology should permit, warm to the touch, and they can't survive on ordinary food alone. Without a supplemental ration of their master's blood every few weeks, their body starts consuming itself.

The relationship is where the design pays off. Turned serve, most of them for centuries, and a few break free while most don't. They're warriors, stewards, advisors, secret-keepers. In many clans they're treated as kin despite the formal hierarchy, and that's not sentimentality papering over servitude — it's a household where the servants cannot leave for reasons nobody has to enforce.

What makes it playable rather than merely grim is that the bond runs both directions. A Turned is a permanent obligation. The master has to keep configuring the link, keep providing, keep them viable. Neglect isn't cruelty with no consequences; it's the first step toward producing something that will have to be caged or destroyed. An Obur with several Turned is running a small dependent household with a metabolic budget.

When it breaks, it usually kills one or both parties, and the more common direction is the one nobody plans for: the master dies of something unrelated, and every Turned they made loses their capabilities in the same instant. That's not a betrayal or a punishment. It's just what happens when the thing you were plugged into stops existing — and it's where the Starved come from.
