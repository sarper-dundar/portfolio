# Tünkurkan — The Plane of Emotion and Free Will

Kazankor has a cosmology full of machinery. Tözyar holds the laws in place. Tünmorin recycles the dead on schedule. The gods run a harvest on a timetable measured in tens of thousands of years. In a universe built like that, the obvious question is whether anybody actually chooses anything.

Tünkurkan is the answer, and it's an answer with an address. Free will isn't an assumption in this setting or a philosophical position. It's a plane, maintained by beings whose job is maintaining it, which means it's a thing that could in principle be damaged.

The plane governs two linked domains: the whole emotional spectrum, and the capacity to make choices. It's the reason feelings exist and the reason those feelings translate into decisions. Its rule is that even in a universe with fate and cosmic order in it, the choices a being makes still determine the course of their life.

Its inhabitants split cleanly. The **Emissaries of Will** observe and protect the power of choice throughout the universe. They're impartial and they never interfere with a decision — they exist to make sure no being is forced to act against its own desires, which is a very different job from making sure beings choose well. The **Empaths** are the caretakers, maintaining emotional balance, nurturing what's positive and easing suffering.

The owner, **Bayunir, the Heart of Choice**, is the warmest being in my cosmology and I wrote him that way on purpose. He isn't a god set over the plane — a Plane Owner is their plane expressed as a being, so Tünkurkan doesn't obey Bayunir, it _is_ Bayunir. His form shifts with what he governs — sometimes a glowing figure radiating warmth, sometimes a shadowy contemplative one embodying sorrow or doubt, eyes changing colour with the emotional state of the universe. He's approachable. He listens. He can show a person the true nature of a choice before they make it, which is one of the few genuinely helpful powers any plane owner has.

And he will not protect anyone from the outcome. Bayunir believes absolutely in consequence. He does not shield beings from the results of their decisions, cannot control those outcomes, and won't override a choice once it's made even when it's tragic. That combination — real compassion, zero rescue — is the most useful god-shaped thing I have. Players can go to Bayunir for clarity and they will get it, and they will get nothing else.

If he's weakened or distracted, emotions spiral. People act impulsively, feelings intensify past what anyone can carry, and the plane becomes chaotic. A world where the free will plane is malfunctioning doesn't look like tyranny. It looks like everybody making terrible decisions at once.

For practitioners, Tünkurkan grants emotional amplification and influence, and it charges in the obvious currency: emotional memories, specific emotions, emotional connections, and at the deeper tiers free will and personal choice themselves. Paying a Heavy sacrifice here can mean giving up your own capacity to decide something.

The mechanic I'm proudest of is the **Emotional Feedback Loop**. Every time a practitioner amplifies a particular emotion, they accumulate points in it, and after enough repetition they acquire the trait. Lean on courage often enough and you get a courageous heart — advantage against fear, and a growing tendency to act recklessly. Lean on calm and you get an unyielding calm that also makes you seem distant. Rage gives you damage and a temper you can't hold. Sorrow gives you real insight into grief and a penalty to every social roll you make.

Keep going and the trait becomes permanent, reversible only through a specific ritual or long abstinence from that emotion entirely.

I like this because it makes the plane's own subject matter into its cost structure. A Tünkurkan practitioner doesn't get drained. They get _shaped_. The character who solved every problem for two years by amplifying courage is now genuinely a reckless person, and nobody made them that way except their own casting habits. It's the closest thing in my systems to a character arc that emerges from play rather than from a player's intention.

There's a lighter version too — **Empathic Echoes**, where certain spells leave a link between caster and target for a few hours, both feeling what the other feels. Positive states help. Negative states make both parties irritable and worse at Wisdom saves. Conflicting states leave both disoriented. It's a small rule and it produces a lot of accidental roleplay.

The tradeoff, which is real: a system that changes a player's character without asking needs a table that wants that. I check before anyone commits to this plane. A player who signed up to play a specific person and finds themselves slowly becoming someone else has a legitimate complaint, and "the plane did it" isn't a good enough answer if they never agreed to the premise.
