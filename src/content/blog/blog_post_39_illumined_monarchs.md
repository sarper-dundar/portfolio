# The Illumined Monarchs — Ascended Mortals

Becoming an Illumined Monarch takes two things: reaching level 30, and a **god seed**.

That's the whole requirement, and the important part is what isn't in it. It doesn't say Alchemist. Kazankor has no class gatekeeping anywhere else in the setting, and I'm not going to introduce it at the very top of the ladder. A Ruh Usta with ten rings, a Tamga master, a Sülde bender who has spent a lifetime with their Iye, a Songs practitioner holding something complete — any of them can ascend, if they get to thirty and get their hands on a seed.

What Alchemists have isn't permission. It's supply. An Alchemist who walks their path far enough can **create** a god seed. The transformation that ends their progression produces the exact thing everyone else has to go and find, which makes theirs the only route to godhood that doesn't depend on luck.

I like that distinction a great deal, because it's the same shape as every other access question in this world. Nothing is locked by what you are; things are locked by whether you can get the material. The Alchemist's advantage over a Ruh Usta isn't that the door is open to one and shut to the other. It's that one of them is searching and the other is manufacturing.

For an Alchemist, the process is the end of a very long transformation. Every tier before the last involves True Source rewriting the body toward something — a direction set by the formula, shaped by ingredients, held in place by Digestion Conditions. At the end that relationship inverts. The body stops being shaped by True Source and becomes something that shapes it. They aren't channeling a field anymore; they _are_ a True Source event, leaving field impressions behind them as they move. When they're angry, nearby Tamutash warms. When they sleep, other Alchemists feel the echo in their own Corruption.

What ascension looks like by the other route — thirty levels of something else, plus a seed found rather than made — is thinner in my notes, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. The destination is the same. The road there isn't decades of the body being rewritten, so a Monarch who arrived that way probably doesn't wear it the way an Alchemist does. That's a real gap and it's near the top of my list.

What is constant is that any Monarch recognises another instantly, without a check, regardless of concealment. They sense each other's fields, and it is unmistakable.

Most are older than they look, and most aren't from the previous cycle. They survived earlier resets — which requires hiding from a collapse, causing it to fail locally, or being embedded deeply enough in the True Source field that it simply didn't reach you. Some are from cycles distant enough that their memories of the world they were born in aren't reliable, not because memory failed but because that world has left no physical evidence of having existed.

They have four things in common. They've watched the Luminous Ones make the same decisions more than once. They've accumulated knowledge across cycles no current institution can match. They've learned to be careful about what they reveal and to whom. And every one of them has watched people they cared about die in a reset, and has had to decide what that means for how they engage with anyone now.

Everything else differs. They don't form a faction, don't share goals, and don't agree. Some work openly for Luminous Ones as valued agents. Some spend their existence trying to undermine the reset cycle. Some have withdrawn into private projects — building things, studying things, keeping up a relationship with one place or idea across several cycles. Some are content with what they are. Some are not.

Most conceal themselves, and not out of fear. It's calculation: a publicly known Monarch draws attention, which draws questions, which draws the scrutiny that makes whatever they're actually doing harder.

The relationship with the Luminous Ones is the part I find most useful in play, because it isn't hostility. The gods have always known Monarchs exist. Resets aren't designed to kill them specifically — they're designed to collapse a civilization — so a survivor is an acceptable loss condition rather than a threat to the mechanism.

What the gods calculate is whether a particular Monarch is worth moving against, and the cost is real. A Monarch can hurt a god. Not kill one — the asymmetry of divine existence prevents that under normal circumstances — but damage them in ways that matter, delay them at real expense, and create problems requiring serious investment to fix. No Luminous One spends that unless the Monarch threatens something they care about.

The result is mutual deterrence expressed as formal politeness. Both sides are courteous. Both sides are counting.

Which answers the question the premise raises. The gods allow ascension because ascended mortals mostly work for them, and stopping it would cost more than tolerating it. A Monarch who keeps quiet and pursues their own projects isn't a problem worth solving. The path to the top of the world is open, unguarded, and watched — and the only real bottleneck on it is a seed.
