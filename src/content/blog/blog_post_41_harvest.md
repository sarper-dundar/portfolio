# The Harvest — What the Gods Are Actually Doing

I've described the premise before: the gods farm this world, engineer a civilizational war every ten to twenty thousand years, harvest what the war produced, and let a new civilization grow on the ruins. That's the summary. This post is about the mechanism, because the mechanism is what makes it hold together.

It starts with a property of the world itself. Kazankor's atmosphere, exposed to the True Source, produces **Tamutash** — and only Kazankor's does. Every use of True Source here creates a minuscule, dust-sized quantity of an indestructible material that gathers over millennia into ore, and once excavated it holds the power of the True Source permanently without deteriorating. That's what makes Tamga inscription possible at all.

So the world is a factory whose output scales with how much magic gets used inside it, and that single fact generates the entire divine arrangement. If yield tracks usage, the way to increase yield is to increase usage — and nothing makes people spend power desperately, wastefully, and at scale like a war for survival. The gods don't engineer wars because they enjoy conflict. They engineer wars because war is the highest-throughput production method available. Every siege is manufacturing.

Which reframes the reset, and this is the part I'd most want to get right, because it isn't what it looks like. **The gods do not intend to end the world.** They intend a war large enough to spike production. Total annihilation is not the goal and is not useful to them — a dead world grows no new civilization to harvest next time.

The wars spiral out of control anyway. Every single time. The Luminous Ones start a conflict calibrated for yield, it consumes the civilization producing the yield, and then they collect what was made and wait for the next one to grow. That isn't a plan working. It's a plan failing the same way for tens of thousands of years, run by people powerful enough that failing costs them nothing. Between resets they hold a cold-war equilibrium, waiting for civilizations to get large enough to be worth setting against each other. The Forgotten Arteries exist because this has happened repeatedly and each cycle's cities are still down there in layers.

And the Tamutash goes to the gods, who use it to inscribe Tamga. The most learnable magic in the world, the one any patient person with a teacher can pick up, is the same craft the harvest exists to supply. Mortals are working with the offcuts of their own extraction.

The constraint that keeps it stable is the interesting part. Tamutash is unmatched, so Kazankor draws attention from gods across the cosmos — and that attention produced an unspoken agreement: **none may dominate or unite the world, and none may lay full claim to it.** They influence it through believers instead, and make sure it never grows too powerful.

That isn't a moral restraint. It's a cartel. Any god who took Kazankor outright would face all the others, so nobody takes it and everybody works to keep the asset productive and unclaimed.

I find this more useful than a villain because it explains things a villain can't. Why is the world always at war but never conquered? Conquest breaks the arrangement. Why does progress keep stalling? It gets reset when technology grows too advanced. Why do the gods answer prayers? Believers are how influence gets applied without a claim. The one thing they don't permit is unity, because a unified Kazankor might work out what's happening and stop supplying.

It also explains something I only recently followed through. A farm needs stock. If yield scales with how many people are spending power, an underpopulated Kazankor is an underperforming one — so the gods take species and whole races from other worlds and put them here to fill the place out.

The Obur are the documented case, caught elsewhere in the cosmos and brought here half a million years ago, with everything they now believe about themselves a distorted memory of arriving. They aren't special, though. They're an example. Some share of the peoples on this world were imported to it, and none of them remember, because enough cycles have passed that no origin story survives intact.

It isn't only that the gods farm this world. They stock it.

Two things have already gone wrong. The Sönmezler worked it out forty thousand years ago and tried to become unharvestable; the experiment succeeded incorrectly, and divine agents were in the Arteries within a century. They are proof that the pattern is detectable and that detecting it is not the same as escaping it. And the higher-order Iye know — every reset scatters their gatherings and forces them to re-accumulate over millennia, and they remember. They are the only faction with both a real grievance and a form the gods can't easily suppress.

What I want the harvest to do for a campaign is remove the shape of story where the party wins. There's no throne to take and no dark lord to kill. There's an arrangement between powers, and the players can understand it, work around it, exploit it, join it, or spend their lives trying to damage it — and any of those is a full campaign.

The questions it leaves open are the ones I want left open. Why did this start? Did all the gods agree, or were some outvoted? And the one above all of it: the Üçler made this world and the Atalar maintain the rules it runs on, and neither tier appears to have any opinion about a resource operation running inside their creation. I haven't decided whether that's approval, inattention, or a timescale on which it hasn't happened yet.
