# Long Rest = 7 Days

In Kazankor a short rest is one day and a long rest is seven. I changed this early, and it has turned out to be the most far-reaching rule I've written — further-reaching than removing the spell system, because the rest cadence touches every other system at once.

The problem is the one every 5e table knows. "Once per long rest" is meant to mark a significant ability used at a significant moment. In practice it means once per nap. A party that can rest whenever it likes has abilities that recharge whenever it likes, so the only interesting question — spend it now or hold it — stops being a question. The fiction suffers too. A character who took a spear through the lung is fine tomorrow.

Seven days fixes it by making rest expensive in the one currency that can't be farmed. If you want your pool back, a week passes, and the world moves during that week.

What I didn't expect was how much work the change would do elsewhere. Once a long rest is a week, the week becomes the unit of everything. D&D has always measured downtime in weeks; it just never lined up with rest, so downtime lived in a separate ledger most tables ignore. Now it's the same clock. In a given week a character can take a long rest or run an active downtime project, not both, unless the project is passive. That one constraint does more scheduling work than any table I've written.

Injuries gained weight almost for free. The wound tables already described recovery in rests — an opened thigh heals in two long rests, a properly set broken leg in six. Read those as two weeks and six weeks and they stop being abstractions. A character who loses a fight is out of the action for a stretch the party has to plan around.

Training landed in the same place. A new skill proficiency is four weeks with a teacher; expertise is eight weeks with a master, and finding a master is usually its own side quest. A single Song is three to six weeks with someone who already knows it. None of that needed special pleading once the week was the natural grain of the calendar.

The resource systems agreed without argument. Sülde returns to a full pool, a Ruh Usta's soul power to maximum, and Songs of Creation clear all accumulated Strain and Fracture. A **Kök Söz** speaker manages a handful of deep workings per long rest, and a mortal who reaches past their ceiling may need months. One rule, four shapes.

The cost is real. You cannot run a dungeon crawl the way 5e expects. A party that spends its resources in the second of five rooms chooses between pushing on depleted and leaving to return in a week, to a dungeon that has noticed. That's good tension when I've built for it and miserable when I haven't. It also means I have to actually run downtime; a DM who hand-waves the week between sessions gets the awkwardness and none of the benefit.

The part I like most came last. It makes the calendar an opponent. Kharos-Vaul has a schedule of things that happen whether or not the party is ready, and every long rest spends a week against it. Resting became a decision with a price, which is what I wanted "once per long rest" to mean in the first place.
