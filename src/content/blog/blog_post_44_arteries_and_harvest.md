# The Arteries and the Harvest

Every setting with a hidden premise faces the same question: how does anyone ever find out? The usual answers are a prophecy, an ancient text, or a helpful immortal, and all three have the same weakness — they're a person telling the players something, which means the players are receiving information rather than discovering it.

Kazankor's answer is that the evidence is geological. The world keeps its own records, in order, and anyone willing to dig can read them.

The Forgotten Arteries are civilizational strata. Floor 1 holds pieces of the current age. Floor 2 is a global machine civilization twenty thousand years dead. Floor 3 is the Bright Age, forty thousand down. Below that, the Age of Wells, and deeper still. Each layer is a complete world with its own technology, its own architecture, its own dead, sitting on top of the one before it.

Nobody has to explain the harvest to a party that has walked through three of them. The pattern is the floor plan.

What makes it evidence rather than atmosphere is the _regularity_. One dead civilization is a tragedy. Two is history. Five, spaced at roughly consistent intervals, each ending at its technological peak rather than in decline, is a **schedule** — and a schedule implies somebody keeping it. That inference is available to any player who thinks about what they're standing in, and it arrives without an NPC saying a word.

The details reinforce it in ways I didn't have to plan, because they follow from the premise. Every one of these civilizations was at its height when it stopped. Floor 2 was a global culture at peak. Floor 3 had spent twenty thousand years thriving on raw divine energy and understood the harvest better than anyone alive today. These are not societies that decayed. They're societies that were doing well and then abruptly weren't, which is what a harvest looks like from underneath and what a natural collapse never looks like.

The Machine Age gives the sharpest single piece. Its civilization was building something unfinished — a project needing a mineral from Floor 4, a technique from Floor 3, and design documents from Floor 5, all collected from the floors _below_ them. So the Machine Age was excavating the ruins of the Bright Age the same way the current age excavates theirs, working downward through the same layers, reaching for something and running out of time. Their Central AI has been waiting twenty thousand years for someone to finish the work.

Which means the current civilization is not the first to notice the pattern. It's at least the third. Everyone who has ever dug into these tunnels has found older tunnels and drawn conclusions, and every one of them was interrupted on the same schedule that produced the layers they were reading.

Floor 3 makes it explicit. The Sönmezler didn't infer the harvest from strata — they lived through enough of it to understand the cycle directly, and they tried to become unharvestable rather than warn anyone. They failed in the specific way I described earlier, and they've been hiding forty thousand years since. They are still down there, in the layer their own civilization became, which is about as pointed as I can make it: the people who understood the system best are now part of the sediment.

The design principle I'd take from this into any other setting is that a premise held only in the metaphysics is a premise the players receive. A premise expressed in the terrain is one they can find. The harvest isn't a secret because it's hidden. It's a secret because it's beneath everyone, in a place most people have no reason to enter and good reason to avoid.

That also explains the surface. People in the current age aren't stupid for not knowing. The evidence is real, complete, and unhidden — and it's under several miles of tunnels full of hostile inscription arrays, machines with grudges, spore kingdoms, and things that have been waiting alone for forty thousand years. The reason nobody knows isn't suppression. It's that the archive is a dungeon, and reading it costs what dungeons cost.

The last thing the Arteries do is set the stakes without me having to state them. A party standing on Floor 3 is standing in the answer to the question the campaign is about, and looking down at floors they haven't reached. Whatever is under the deepest layer they've seen is older than any civilization that has left a record, and it's still down there, and the cycle that buried all of it has not stopped running.

They're currently living on the top layer.
