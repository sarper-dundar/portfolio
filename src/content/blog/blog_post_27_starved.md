# The Starved

A Turned Obur's body was rebuilt to run on two things: blood, and a channeling link to the Obur who made them. The Starved are what happens when the second one fails and the first requirement doesn't.

That's the whole creature. They keep the reshaped physiology — still adapted for blood-processing, still warmer than mortal, still running at a metabolic intensity that shouldn't be biologically possible. They lose the channeling that powered it. So the body starts consuming itself to make up the difference, and it demands blood at the rate of a fully functional Turned while having none of the capabilities that let a Turned acquire blood sensibly. What's left is a predatory animal in the shape of someone who used to be a person. They feed compulsively. They cannot be reasoned with.

There are three roads in and they all end the same way.

The first is insufficient sustenance — a Turned whose conduit was always marginal, either from an imperfect Turning or a master who never granted them a strong configuration. They operate at the edge of viability for months or years, drawing less than they need, consuming their own body to compensate, until the conduit closes and the mind breaks under constant deprivation. Some of them see it happening and go to their master for help. A wise master can reconfigure the link and stop the slide. A neglectful or punishing one lets it finish.

The second is the common one and the one I find genuinely sad: the master dies. Not betrayal, not punishment — an unrelated death somewhere else, and every Turned that Obur made loses their capability in the same moment. The body still needs what the bond was supplying. A severed Turned has weeks to months before the slide starts, depending on what blood they can find by other means, and finding another Obur willing to feed them is rare and hunting enough mortal blood alone is both rare and dangerous. Most can't. Once the slide begins it's fast: rising hunger, falling cognition, identity draining out over a few weeks.

The third is deliberate. Some masters make Starved on purpose — to produce a controllable weapon, to punish serious betrayal, to clear an inheritance in clans where Turned hold influence over wealth, or occasionally because the master is simply cruel. A deliberately-Starved Turned is often more useful than a naturally-Starved one, because the master tuned the process to preserve a thread of the original binding, and can sometimes issue commands. The control is unreliable, and a Starved will turn on its master like it turns on anything else when the control slips.

The reason the Starved work as the campaign's ambient dread rather than as a monster is that nothing about them is anyone's plan. The most common Starved in the world is the byproduct of an ordinary death. Somewhere an Obur died of a fall or an illness or a knife, and three people in another city stopped being able to do what they could do that morning, and have a few weeks to sort it out. The clans keep theirs caged or contained. Some release them as last-resort shock troops, and most don't, because loosing Starved into mortal territory carries severe political consequences and rarely buys enough to be worth it.

So the Starved aren't a faction and they don't have a leader or an agenda. They're a failure mode with a population. When one turns up in a village it isn't a plot — it's the downstream consequence of an event that happened somewhere else, to someone the party will never hear about.

The other thing they do, structurally, is make the Obur legible. A party that meets a Starved before it meets an Obur has learned the important fact first: this power comes with a dependency, and dependencies break. Every Obur the players later meet is walking around with a small number of people whose continued personhood is contingent on that Obur staying alive. I don't have to explain the stakes of the Turned relationship. They've seen the other end of it.
