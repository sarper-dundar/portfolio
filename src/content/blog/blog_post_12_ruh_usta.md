# Ruh Usta — Soul Crystallization

Most people's souls are shapeless — a diffuse inner light that does nothing in particular. In a **Ruh Usta**, the soul has crystallized into something specific, and it isn't chosen. It's discovered.

The reason I built the system is narrow and practical. Every other power source in Kazankor is detectable, and a world where the gods are running an operation needs one kind of strength that doesn't show up on their instruments. Ruh Usta abilities register as nothing. They can't be detected as magical, can't be suppressed by antimagic, and don't interfere with any magic system the character learns separately. Walk a Ruh Usta into a sealed anti-magic vault and they're exactly as dangerous inside it as out.

Four soul types, set at awakening and permanent. **Canavar Ruhu** is a beast soul expressing through the body, with the specific creature deciding your whole approach to a fight — a bear soul wrestles, a spider soul controls space. **Silah Ruhu** is a weapon made of pure self that can't be taken, dropped by force, or destroyed. **Unsur Ruhu** is elemental, born with one element too close to the core. **Destek Ruhu** projects a form with no combat capability at all; every ring ability it gains goes outward, to allies or enemies, never to the Ruh Usta. Its bonuses are flat numbers by absolute rule, never percentages. I expect almost nobody to play it, and it exists because a system should have one role that only works if you actually want it.

The elemental soul does something deliberate. An Unsur and a Sülde bender both throw fire and they are opposites. Sülde asks the Iye to move; an Unsur shoves the element where they want it through will alone. So the Unsur controls only their innate element, has shorter range, and can't commune with Iye at all — the Iye withdraw, because they can feel it's a dominance relationship. In exchange they do about half again the damage. Domination is more destructive than cooperation, and it closes every door cooperation opens.

Advancement runs on rings. The soul grows by absorbing the essence of defeated creatures, each absorption producing a **Ruh Halkası** that bonds permanently. Acquisition happens in the window after a creature hits zero and before it actually dies — whoever lands the killing blow takes the ring, one ring to one person, and if the creature bleeds out on its own the ring is lost. That single rule turns every significant kill into a group decision.

Rings draw on 47 categories, from burst damage and restraint through flight and regeneration up to a small reality-bending set covering phase, time dilation, gravity and negation. What a ring becomes depends on your soul rather than the creature: the same spider gives a Canavar a strike that restrains on hit, gives a Silah a thread technique that launches and retracts the weapon, and gives a Destek a web zone that slows enemies. The soul filters rather than rejects.

The resource gives the system its texture. Soul power is **bidirectional** — it has an optimal zone and constantly pressures itself back toward it. Above the ceiling you get up to +3 on attacks and saves, but the surplus bleeds off at roughly 7% of maximum per turn, giving a consistent four-to-five-round window at every level. Below the floor, abilities start costing 10%, then 25%, then 50% more. Go past zero and your maximum drops; severe damage takes half the pool and a long rest won't touch it.

The honest cost of all this is that the system advances by killing things, which shapes what a Ruh Usta wants from a session in a way I can't fully steer. A player carrying an empty ring slot has a reason to escalate. Sometimes that's the engine of a good arc, and sometimes it's why a negotiation goes wrong. I've learned to plan for both.
