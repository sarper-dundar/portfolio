# Kök Söz

The five magic systems are what Kazankor knows about. **Kök Söz** — "Root-Word" — is the sixth, and it isn't in the public count because almost nobody in the world knows it exists. There is no school of it, no scholar of it, no academy. That isn't institutional secrecy. It's that the thing cannot be taught.

Kök Söz is the language reality was named into being with. Every other system works inside reality: Tamga binds contracts onto its surface, Songs re-tune it, Outsource borrows from elsewhere in it. Kök Söz addresses the structure that lets reality be what it is, which is why it reaches things nothing else can — souls, identity, the past, causality, the count of what exists.

The beings whose nature _is_ creation speak it natively. For them it isn't knowledge, it's being, which is exactly why they can't hand it over. Everyone else, Luminous Ones included, can only imitate — **Taklit**. A mortal throat cannot produce the phonemes and a mortal mind cannot hold the grammar, so every imitator's approximation is unique to their own body. There is no correct pronunciation to learn, which means there can be no school, which means the whole thing stays a rumour.

What an imitator holds isn't a language. It's **concepts** — individual cosmic operations someone handed them. They aren't categories in any mortal sense; not Fire, not Force, not Binding, because those are our taxonomies and they don't exist in the language. They're operations like _sever the relation between an act and its consequence_, or _hold a moment as if it were a place_. Whoever holds one carries something they cannot understand at the level its native speakers do.

Each concept is tracked by two numbers, and separating them is what made the system work. **Derinlik** is scope, one to nine — how much of reality the working reaches, from a small object and a few seconds up to effects that propagate beyond Kazankor. **Peril** is the inherent danger of the operation, rated +0 to +6, and it belongs to the concept rather than the speaker. It does not move with scope.

Keeping those apart is what stops the math collapsing. "Withdraw one small coin from the count of what exists" is a tiny working — one coin — that carries catastrophic Peril, because the danger is in what the operation _does_, not how large it is. The temptation is to let dangerous things climb the scope ladder. Instead danger has its own axis, and a perilous concept is hard to overreach with safely at any size at all.

Then there's **Kavrayış**, comprehension, a percentage at the speaker's current tier. It does two jobs. It sets effective reach — Derinlik plus Kavrayış over a hundred, so Derinlik 4 at 80% gives an effective 4.8. And it governs quality: at 70% and up a working does exactly and only what was intended; from 30 to 69% it lands with rough edges; below 30% it succeeds and _spills_, catching things it wasn't aimed at.

That produces a result I like. A speaker freshly promoted to Derinlik 5 at 0% and one maxed out at Derinlik 4 with 100% have identical reach. The promoted one's workings are crude and leaky; the maxed one's are clean. Reach and control are different things, and the system says so out loud.

Resolution is a gap: attempted scope minus effective tier. Zero or less and it simply lands, no roll. Positive and you roll a Break check against 10, plus twice the gap, plus the concept's Peril, minus a flat tolerance for what kind of being you are — six for a Luminous One, four for an Illumined Monarch, two for a Yarımca, nothing for an ordinary mortal.

A Break is never just damage. Transformative failure is the rule, and it should match the working that failed: a ruined throat, voided memories, soul scarring other practitioners can see, identity drifting toward whatever the working reached for, someone you love who no longer knows you. At the top, the half-happened working stays in the region and spreads — which is where the setting's dead zones and wrong places come from.

Which brings me to the **Mortal Ceiling**, the piece I'd defend hardest. No mortal can cleanly land a working of Derinlik 8 or 9, whatever their comprehension. They don't roll. It automatically Breaks, and the Break _is_ the cosmic effect, half-firing as it tears loose and rewriting the speaker on its way out.

The problem it solves is one every high-power system has. Output here is universal by design — the same working does the same thing whether a god or a farmer said it. So what stops a desperate mortal ending a continent? Not a permission check. The bomb does the same damage regardless of who pressed the button; what differs is what happens to the person pressing it. A mortal can still set the device off. They cannot do it without it going off in their hands.

The last rule keeps it out of character builds entirely: nothing grows on its own. A concept sits where it arrived until I move it as a consequence of something that happened in the story, and a fresh tier always starts at low comprehension — newly able to reach further, not yet able to control it.

So Kök Söz is the one system in Kazankor that isn't a player option. It's a threat with rules, and the rules exist so that when it appears it behaves consistently rather than however I feel that evening. That's a lot of mechanism for something no player can pursue. I think it's the right trade; the alternative is a setting where the most fundamental force in the cosmos resolves by fiat.
